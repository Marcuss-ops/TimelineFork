## Hook formula

The first 30 seconds do four things:

1. Start with an ordinary object: “These look like ordinary photos…”
2. Imply the turn: “…but what happened right after these pictures were taken made them terrifying.”
3. Promise three concrete examples: a Snapchat selfie, a party photo, a recovered memory card.
4. Set an escalation: “We’ll start with the shocking cases and work our way to the most disturbing.”

That is a clear contract with the viewer: ordinary object, hidden consequence, multiple cases, increasing intensity.

## Case formula

Each case follows a repeatable spine:

- A one-sentence hook that states the ordinary situation and the disaster.
- Context: who the people are, where they are, what they wanted.
- A turn: the detail that changes the meaning of the ordinary scene.
- Chronology: short beats, each one adding a fact.
- A false resolution or a surprise: “But the case was far from over.”
- The real resolution: arrest, conviction, identification, or an unanswered question.
- A bridge: “This next story is about…”

The transitions are explicit. The viewer is never lost. The narrator repeatedly uses “But,” “Then,” “Sadly,” and “What happened next…” as gear changes.

## Style choices worth borrowing

- Short sentences. One idea per sentence.
- Concrete nouns: camera, memory card, belt, selfie, window, field guide.
- Second person and direct address: “We’ll look at…”, “My friends, pay attention…”
- Open loops: “There was a dark surprise coming.”
- Recap at the end: the ordinary moment, the click, the consequence.
- A final lesson that is simple and portable: pay attention to small details.

## What not to borrow

- The 195 wpm pace is too fast for a slow-burn forensic documentary.
- The sponsor break interrupts the escalation.
- Some claims are asserted without sourcing. “Secret Diaries” should keep the documented/legend distinction visible.
- The reference relies on shocking cases. This script should rely on the record, not on reenactment.

## Application to “Secret Diaries”

- Keep the cold-open promise, but make it stranger: eight records, six documented, two legends.
- Use the case spine, but slow it down to 155–175 wpm.
- Target 7,000–7,400 narration words for 40–45 minutes.
- End each case with a question, not a summary.
- Use explicit bridges: “The next record was not a diary. It was a letter.”
- Make the fact-check a feature of the narration: “Here is where I have to be honest with you.”
- Avoid the reference’s rapid-fire countdown. The pleasure here is accumulation, not speed.
